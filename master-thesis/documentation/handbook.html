From an observation to a forecast target

Define the data, timing, and units before interpreting any model output.

The experiment evaluates four daily equity-index series and one nonfinancial Sunspot robustness series. Adjusted financial closing levels are transformed into log returns. Sunspot targets remain native values, so the two kinds of tasks require different unit interpretations.

A forecast origin identifies when a prediction is made. The financial target is the sum of future returns over one or five observations; the four lag positions instead identify past inputs. A level trend alone does not demonstrate predictability of future returns.

EVALUATION DESIGN

Time boundaries and fair evaluation

Follow the information available to fitting, preprocessing, testing, and explanation.

The supervised frames remove the 252-lag warm-up and the final rows needed to construct the longest target. Feature scaling uses each training interval. The outer design separates supervised training and testing with five purge rows and five embargo rows.

Static models have six native forecast folds per scheme, while neural models have four. Neural sequence construction excludes 21 initial test positions, leaving 231 forecasts from a 252-row block. The primary seven-model comparison retains their common origin intersection: 924 origins in each of 20 settings.

Fixed explanation probes form a separate evaluation sample. Holding an input fixed across consecutive fitted folds makes explanation changes interpretable as changes under refitting, rather than merely changes of input. Shared probes and related indices still limit independence.

LIVE CALCULATION

The metric microscope

Explore how magnitude, direction, and reference benchmarks can disagree.

RMSE and MAE summarize error magnitude differently. Directional accuracy measures financial sign agreement. R² compares loss with the realized evaluation-mean reference. Relative RMSE compares the model with persistence. These quantities are complementary rather than interchangeable.

The teaching lab starts from recorded matched forecasts. Changing a multiplier or additive bias creates a hypothetical transformation while keeping the realized targets fixed. Recomputed metrics describe that edited set of predictions, not a newly fitted research model.

XAI & FAITHFULNESS

What can an explanation prove?

Separate numerical reconstruction, intervention behavior, and economic meaning.

The primary channels are exact Linear attributions, native ARIMA lag counterfactuals, complete-ensemble TreeSHAP, and KernelSHAP summed into four lag groups for the neural models. Native TFT selection weights remain a distinct inspection channel.

Additivity checks reconstruction of a fitted predictor. Deletion and insertion compare intervention effects with equivalent random feature-group orders. PFI alters inputs through shuffling. The LIME-style routine evaluates a weighted surrogate against model outputs on perturbed inputs. Agreement across these diagnostics is not guaranteed.

The available variables are lagged returns or native Sunspot values. Their attribution magnitudes cannot identify a macroeconomic or causal mechanism. ALE belongs to the theoretical discussion, but the provided executed artifacts contain no ALE curve results.

TEMPORAL ROBUSTNESS

Does the explanation survive refitting?

Compare fixed-probe vectors and identify what each agreement measure retains or discards.

The temporal evaluation compares signed four-lag attribution vectors at the same probe across consecutive fitted folds. Spearman correlation compares ranks; cosine compares orientation; the top-feature indicator compares the largest absolute contribution.

Positive rescaling leaves both Spearman correlation and cosine unchanged even when total attribution magnitude changes greatly. With four features, rankings are coarse; ties and constant vectors require explicit handling. High pooled medians can obscure poor tails or sparse regime strata.

Temporal refitting, changing a fitting seed, and resampling an explainer are different perturbations. The limited tree-seed analysis does not substitute for repeated neural fits or repeated fixed-model explanation sampling.

STATISTICAL INFERENCE

From loss differences to evidence claims

Distinguish descriptive error reductions from adjusted inferential evidence.

The synthesis uses observation-level squared-loss differentials at common origins. Autocovariances remain within contiguous forecast blocks; the implemented variance uses an unweighted sum through horizon h minus one.

Seven models create 21 unordered pairs in a selected setting. Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment operates within that family. Supplementary native model–benchmark tests instead use a 14-test primary family and a separately reported two-test sensitivity analysis.

An exploratory threshold changes which adjusted values pass a displayed decision rule. It does not rewrite the thesis’s q = 0.05 conclusion. A significance claim also needs the loss direction, coverage definition, and practical effect size.

Seven model families

Linear

Linear Regression

A visible weighted sum of four standardized lagged inputs.

  1. Construct lag_1, lag_5, lag_21, and lag_252 on the correct target scale.
  2. Fit means and scales using only the fold training interval.
  3. Estimate an intercept and coefficients by ordinary least squares.
  4. Apply the same fitted mapping out of sample and decompose it with exact linear contributions.

What it offers: Compact structure, fast fitting, explicit coefficients, and exact additive reconstruction make the mapping easy to inspect.

Interpretation limits: A linear model may miss nonlinear relationships; correlated predictors also limit the interpretation of an individual coefficient. Neither a coefficient nor an attribution is automatically causal.

Executed configuration: The source uses one seed and six native forecast folds per scheme. Browser OLS reconstruction is checked against all 30,240 native Linear forecasts, with maximum absolute error about 2.6 × 10⁻¹³.

ARIMA

ARIMA

Forecast from an explicit statistical model of history and innovations.

  1. Fit seven candidate orders and select the smallest finite training AIC.
  2. Keep the selected parameters fixed for the fold.
  3. Advance the state through all observed raw rows before each forecast origin.
  4. Sum the next h return forecasts for finance, or select the hth native forecast for Sunspot.

What it offers: The candidate order and fitted statistical structure provide a model-specific way to inspect temporal dependence.

Interpretation limits: Conclusions depend on the candidate set and fitting interval. Selected-lag counterfactual contributions are native model mechanics, not SHAP values or causal explanations.

Executed configuration: Candidates are (1,0,0), (2,0,0), (1,0,1), (2,0,1), (3,0,0), (1,1,0), and (2,1,0). ARIMA has the best mean financial rank, 3.125, in the supplied matched summary.

RandomForest

Random Forest

Combine diverse regression trees through averaging.

  1. Train 100 randomized trees per seed.
  2. Limit maximum depth to 10 and require at least 20 observations in a leaf.
  3. Average the trees within each forest, then average the two seeded forests.
  4. Average TreeSHAP decompositions across the same two predictor constituents.

What it offers: Trees can represent nonlinear relationships and feature interactions without specifying a linear equation for each interaction.

Interpretation limits: Many trees reduce direct transparency. Shared or correlated input information complicates importance interpretation, and randomized fits can yield different attributions.

Executed configuration: The tree pipelines use seeds 20261017 and 20261018. At the study threshold, Random Forest has no significant financial pairwise wins and 15 significant losses.

XGBoost

XGBoost

Add regularized tree corrections to a running prediction.

  1. Reserve a chronological validation tail of at least 50 rows.
  2. Fit at most 200 boosting rounds with depth at most 6 and learning rate 0.05.
  3. Use row and column sampling 0.8, L1 0.1, L2 1.0, and 25-round early stopping.
  4. Average both seeded predictors and their full-ensemble TreeSHAP decompositions.

What it offers: Additive tree models can learn flexible nonlinear interactions while using regularization and validation to constrain fitting.

Interpretation limits: Training fit and computational flexibility do not guarantee a held-out advantage. Explanation quality remains conditional on the actual ensemble and its evaluation probes.

Executed configuration: The primary comparison evaluates the same two-seed predictor at the common 924 origins per selected setting. TreeSHAP explains both constituents, rather than one convenient seed.

LightGBM

LightGBM

Use histogram-based boosting and best-first leaf growth to build an additive predictor efficiently.

  1. Bucket continuous feature values into bins for split calculations.
  2. Grow the leaf with greatest loss reduction subject to configured restrictions.
  3. Apply the executed depth, regularization, learning-rate, and early-stopping settings.
  4. Average the two seeded forecasts and matching TreeSHAP decompositions.

What it offers: Efficient split calculations and flexible tree structure permit nonlinear forecasting with explicit fitting limits.

Interpretation limits: Best-first growth can overfit a small sample without suitable restrictions. Efficient training and a good descriptive rank do not establish a significant benchmark gain.

Executed configuration: The recorded settings include 200 maximum rounds, depth 6, and learning rate 0.05. Its mean relative financial RMSE is approximately 1.002912: lowest among the models, but still above persistence on average.

LSTM

Long Short-Term Memory

Learn a recurrent memory state that selectively retains and updates sequence information.

  1. Construct uninterrupted 21-row histories of four standardized lag channels.
  2. Process those histories with two recurrent layers of 64 hidden units.
  3. Train with Adam and a chronological validation tail, then restore the best validation state.
  4. Apply KernelSHAP to the fitted network and sum signed time-coordinate contributions into four lag groups.

What it offers: A learned recurrent state can encode relationships that depend on input sequences rather than isolated tabular rows.

Interpretation limits: The hidden-state computation is not intrinsically transparent. Sequence alignment, training randomness, and explanation sampling affect interpretation and require separate checks.

Executed configuration: One neural seed, four forecast folds per scheme, lookback 21, batch size 64, and at most 25 epochs with patience 8. A 252-row test block contributes 231 evaluated origins after warm-up.

TFT

Simplified Temporal Fusion Transformer

Reweight lag channels, encode their sequence, and combine temporal representations with attention.

  1. Calculate softmax variable-selection weights over four lag channels.
  2. Reweight the sequence and encode it with a one-layer 64-unit LSTM.
  3. Apply four-head attention and a linear prediction head.
  4. Keep native selection/attention signals separate from lag-grouped KernelSHAP.

What it offers: Native weighting mechanisms offer additional inspection channels alongside post-hoc decompositions.

Interpretation limits: This is the thesis’s compact adapter, not every component of a full production TFT. Neither attention nor variable selection proves faithful explanation or causal influence.

Executed configuration: The adapter uses lookback 21, four attention heads, one neural seed, and the same 25-epoch cap with patience 8. Native weighting and KernelSHAP remain distinct evidence channels.

Terminology & abbreviations

AIC / THESIS APPENDIX

Akaike Information Criterion

A model-selection criterion combining fitted likelihood and the number of estimated parameters. Smaller values are preferred among the comparable fitted candidates.

Example: ARIMA selects the lowest training AIC from seven candidate orders.

A lower AIC does not establish better out-of-sample loss than persistence.

ARIMA / THESIS APPENDIX

Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average

A univariate model family combining dependence on past observations, optional differencing, and past innovations.

Example: ARIMA(2,0,1) uses two AR terms, no differencing, and one MA term.

The selected order and native lag counterfactuals do not identify an economic mechanism.

BH / THESIS APPENDIX

Benjamini–Hochberg

A multiple-testing procedure that adjusts a declared family of raw p values to address false discoveries under its assumptions.

Example: The primary matched analysis adjusts 21 model pairs within each setting.

Different adjustment families answer different testing questions; they are not interchangeable.

DA / THESIS APPENDIX

Directional Accuracy

The proportion of financial forecasts whose predicted sign matches the realized target’s sign. Zero has its own sign in the implementation.

Example: Three matching signs from four targets give DA of 75%.

Directional accuracy ignores error magnitude, transaction costs, and portfolio construction.

DM / THESIS APPENDIX

Diebold–Mariano

A comparison of forecast loss based on the time series of observation-level loss differences between two predictors.

Example: Compare squared errors of Linear and TFT at identical origins.

A descriptive rank and a significance decision are different claims.

HAC / THESIS APPENDIX

Heteroscedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent

A variance-estimation perspective accommodating nonconstant variance and serial dependence. The synthesis uses a block-aware, unweighted autocovariance sum.

Example: At horizon five, autocovariances at lags one through four enter the implemented variance estimate.

The implemented formula is not a Bartlett-weighted Newey–West estimator.

LIME / THESIS APPENDIX

Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations

A family of simpler surrogates used to approximate a complex predictor near an input according to a perturbation and weighting scheme.

Example: The notebooks fit weighted ridge surrogates and assess hold-out approximation MSE.

The executed routine is LIME-style; standard-normal perturbations may create unrealistic input combinations.

LSTM / THESIS APPENDIX

Long Short-Term Memory

A recurrent neural network with gated memory and hidden states that process ordered input sequences.

Example: The executed LSTM uses a 21-step history and two 64-unit layers.

A learned gate is a computational mechanism, not an economic explanation.

MAE / THESIS APPENDIX

Mean Absolute Error

The average absolute difference between predictions and their realized targets, expressed in target units.

Example: Errors of 1 and 3 have MAE 2.

MAE values with different target units should not be pooled directly.

OLS / THESIS APPENDIX

Ordinary Least Squares

Fitting a linear predictor by minimizing the sum of squared training residuals.

Example: The Linear model learns an intercept and four standardized-lag coefficients.

Transparency of the fitted equation does not make its coefficients causal.

PFI / THESIS APPENDIX

Permutation Feature Importance

The change in evaluation loss when a feature or lag channel is shuffled across probe observations.

Example: An MSE increase from 0.010 to 0.014 gives a PFI estimate of 0.004.

Correlated inputs and distribution shifts caused by shuffling can affect interpretation.

RMSE / THESIS APPENDIX

Root Mean Squared Error

The square root of average squared prediction error. Squaring increases the influence of large misses.

Example: Errors of 1 and 3 have RMSE √5, about 2.236.

A smaller descriptive value does not by itself demonstrate a significant improvement.

SHAP / THESIS APPENDIX

SHapley Additive exPlanations

An additive feature-attribution framework whose interpretation depends on the explained predictor, background, and explanation construction.

Example: A baseline and four lag contributions reconstruct an explained prediction.

Additivity and a plausible ranking do not establish causal influence or universal faithfulness.

TFT / THESIS APPENDIX

Temporal Fusion Transformer

A forecasting architecture family using feature-selection and temporal mechanisms. This study implements a simplified LSTM-and-attention adapter.

Example: The adapter reweights four lag channels and uses four attention heads.

Its result should not be described as a test of every component in a full TFT implementation.

XAI / THESIS APPENDIX

Explainable Artificial Intelligence

Methods for inspecting aspects of model behavior and evaluating how informative or dependable those inspections are.

Example: Compare signed attributions, their temporal stability, and intervention diagnostics.

An explanation is not a guarantee of forecast skill, human usefulness, or causal validity.

ML

Machine Learning

Learning a predictive mapping from observed examples rather than specifying every decision rule manually.

Example: Train a tree ensemble from lagged inputs and future-return targets.

A learning algorithm’s flexibility does not replace an appropriate evaluation design.

DL

Deep Learning

Learning layered neural representations, including recurrent and attention-based sequence transformations.

Example: LSTM and the simplified TFT are the two deep comparators.

Greater architectural complexity does not ensure lower held-out error.

RF

Random Forest

An ensemble of randomized decision trees whose numeric outputs are averaged in a regression task.

Example: The study averages two seeded forests of 100 trees each.

An explanation must cover the complete predictor rather than a selected tree or seed.

MSE

Mean Squared Error

The average squared prediction residual, used in fitting, forecasting evaluation, and several explanation diagnostics.

Example: Residuals [1,3] give MSE 5.

Its units are squared target units, unlike RMSE or MAE.

AUC

Area Under the Curve

An area summary of successive intervention losses in this study’s deletion and insertion diagnostics.

Example: Compare model-ranked deletion AUC with the mean from 32 random group orderings.

This is not classification ROC AUC.

ALE

Accumulated Local Effects

A model-agnostic method that summarizes accumulated local prediction changes over observed input regions.

Example: A centered effect curve can describe a fitted model’s response to one feature.

No executed ALE result curves are supplied in the submitted exports.

TSF

Time Series Forecasting file format

The supplied Monash file stores the original T1 series used to verify the execution CSV’s aligned values.

Example: All 11,109 execution rows align with the source TSF without a value mismatch.

The source TSF has a longer date span than the execution subset.

CSV

Comma-Separated Values

A text format for rows and columns used for the source prediction, metric, attribution, and diagnostic exports.

Example: Prediction rows include origin date, target date, realized target, forecast, and benchmarks.

Interpret columns according to the sample and target definitions, not just their file names.

R²

Coefficient of determination

A squared-error comparison with the realized evaluation-sample mean reference.

Example: Model squared error 12 versus reference squared error 10 gives R² = −0.2.

The denominator is distinct from the persistence and supervised training-mean forecasts.

Log return

Logarithmic proportional change

The natural logarithm of the ratio of successive adjusted closing levels. It is additive across observations.

Example: log(102/100) is about 0.019803.

A log return is close to a simple percentage return only for sufficiently small changes.

Lag

An earlier observation used as an input

A lag identifies how far into the past an input comes from relative to its modeled feature row.

Example: Financial lag_5 is a past daily log return, not the future five-session target.

A timing relationship does not establish an economic causal relationship.

Horizon

How far ahead the target is realized

The number of observations between a forecast origin and its target realization.

Example: Financial h = 5 means five trading sessions; Sunspot h = 5 means five days.

Changing horizon changes the target and its overlap with adjacent targets.

Persistence

A transparent reference forecast

Predict zero future cumulative return for financial series, or the currently observed native value for Sunspot.

Example: A relative RMSE of 1.01 means 1% higher RMSE than persistence.

A model can beat another model yet still fail to improve on persistence.

Walk-forward

Chronological repeated evaluation

Fit on earlier observations, evaluate later blocks, and move the information boundary forward through the series.

Example: Each training interval precedes a temporal gap and a future test block.

Chronological splitting alone does not fix incorrectly aligned features or targets.

Rolling window

A fixed-size moving training interval

Advance both ends of the training interval to retain a limited amount of recent history.

Example: The executed rolling fits use the latest 500 available training rows.

Discarding older history can increase adaptation while also increasing fit variability.

Expanding window

A growing training interval

Keep the initial training start fixed and add newly available past observations before later folds.

Example: The initial training interval contains 500 rows and subsequent intervals grow.

A larger sample can include outdated relationships as well as additional information.

Purge

A pre-test separation component

Five frame rows form the first component of the executed gap before a test interval.

Example: Five purge rows plus five embargo rows create a ten-row separation.

The label describes implemented interval arithmetic, not an independent proof of zero leakage.

Embargo

The second pre-test separation component

Five additional frame rows separate supervised training from testing in this particular implementation.

Example: Training ends ten frame rows before the test block begins.

Terminology differs across studies; follow the actual code intervals.

Leakage

Information crossing an impermissible boundary

Preprocessing, fitting, or selection uses information that would not be permitted at the relevant forecast origin.

Example: Estimating a scaler on the complete series lets future observations influence training transformations.

A correct outer split does not automatically validate every inner selection or alignment step.

Matched origins

Shared evaluation timestamps

Forecast origins retained in the intersection of all seven model pipelines for primary comparisons.

Example: Each setting has 924 shared origins per model, forming four blocks of 231.

Matching coverage does not equalize information sets, tuning effort, or model complexity.

Native coverage

The original evaluation sample of a pipeline

Use every forecast observation exported by a model, before the seven-way intersection is imposed.

Example: Static pipelines export 1,512 origins per setting; neural pipelines export 924.

Native model–benchmark tests are separate from matched seven-model rankings.

Probe

An input chosen for explanation evaluation

A fixed input origin explained by several refitted models so explanations can be compared without changing the input.

Example: Static methods use 40 common probes and neural methods use 30.

Explanation probes are not interchangeable with the complete forecast-replay sample.

Attribution

A feature-level contribution or reliance measure

A quantity describing how an input feature participates in an explained output under a specified explanation construction.

Example: A negative signed contribution lowers the output relative to its explanation baseline.

Different channels need not share the same baseline, units, or mathematical interpretation.

Baseline

A context-dependent reference quantity

Forecast benchmarks, explanation expected outputs, and intervention replacement values are different kinds of reference.

Example: Standardized zero corresponds to a training-feature mean, while financial persistence is zero future return.

Name the reference before interpreting a difference or diagnostic.

Faithfulness

Agreement with the explained predictor’s behavior

The extent to which an explanation reflects specified computational or intervention properties of its fitted model.

Example: An additive explanation may reconstruct a prediction while its ranking fails deletion checks.

Being faithful to a model does not mean identifying real economic causes.

Stability

Agreement under a declared perturbation

Compare explanations after a specified change, such as a refitted training fold or a different fitting seed.

Example: Consecutive-fold rank correlation compares fixed probes across successive fitted models.

Temporal, seed, and explanation-sampling stability are different robustness constructs.

Regime

An ex post descriptive stratum

A label assigned using the implemented rolling return and volatility rules with full-series quantile thresholds.

Example: The crisis rule identifies the highest-volatility stratum and overrides prior labels.

These are not independently dated historical crises or real-time trading signals.

Causality

A claim about effects under intervention

An economic causal claim requires an identification design capable of distinguishing a mechanism from association.

Example: A large lag_1 attribution does not establish why a market index moved.

The lag-only observational design cannot identify genuine causal economic drivers.

Black box

A predictor difficult to inspect directly

A model whose full mapping is hard to understand because of many interacting learned components or latent states.

Example: KernelSHAP can inspect an LSTM without replacing its recurrent architecture.

Adding a post-hoc explanation does not make an architecture intrinsically interpretable.

Early stopping

Validation-controlled training duration

Monitor a held-out chronological training tail and retain the best recorded fitting state.

Example: The neural execution allows at most 25 epochs, with patience eight.

The validation tail is distinct from the final test sample; this code adds no extra inner purge or validation-tail refit.

Seed

A pseudorandom initialization setting

Initialize a computational random sequence to repeat a particular randomized path in a compatible environment.

Example: The tree pipelines use seeds 20261017 and 20261018.

Two seeded fits provide limited replication, not a complete uncertainty analysis.

Attention

A learned weighted information combination

Query–key scores determine weights that combine value representations inside a model.

Example: Softmax gives more weight to a larger similarity score.

A learned weight is not automatically a faithful explanation or a causal effect.

Variable selection

Native lag-channel weighting

The simplified TFT learns softmax weights that reweight its four input channels before sequence encoding.

Example: The four weights are nonnegative and sum to one.

These internal weights are evaluated separately from KernelSHAP attributions.

Overfitting

Learning sample-specific patterns that fail to generalize

A predictor captures noise or idiosyncratic structure in the fitting data instead of dependable predictive relationships.

Example: Training loss improves while later held-out loss deteriorates.

Apparent training fit does not establish generalization.

Non-stationarity

Changing distributional properties over time

Statistical relationships, variability, or other distributional properties need not remain fixed across a series.

Example: A mapping fitted in one market period can behave differently in a later period.

Descriptive regime labels do not amount to a complete structural-break analysis.

Feature dependence

Shared statistical information between inputs

Predictors can be correlated or related, making independently perturbed combinations unlike naturally observed histories.

Example: Changing one lag while fixing all others can create an unusual input sequence.

Perturbation and importance findings depend on how that dependence is handled.

Explanation background

The reference sample used by an explainer

A set of inputs used to define comparisons or expected outputs for an explanation method.

Example: Deep KernelSHAP uses selected training sequences as its background.

Attributions can depend on the background; they are not unconditional economic truths.

Structural break

A change in a data-generating relationship

A shift in model parameters or distributional behavior that may invalidate a previously fitted relationship.

Example: A coefficient linking a predictor to returns could change after a major market transition.

The experiment’s ex post regime labels are not independently estimated break dates.

Trading profitability

Economic outcome of a specified strategy

A strategy’s returns depend on portfolio rules, position sizing, timing, costs, and risk, beyond a forecast’s sign or numerical error.

Example: A high-DA forecaster can still be unprofitable after costs or large adverse moves.

The study does not infer profitability from DA or explanation plots.
